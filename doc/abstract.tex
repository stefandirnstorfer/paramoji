We are entering an era where computers and AI are becoming increasingly capable
of emotional interaction with humans.
They recognize our emotions and translate them into numerical representations.
Likewise, they encode representations of displayable emotions into behaviors and visual outputs.
This is happening rapidly, with multiple emotional cues being interpreted and exchanged during conversations.

However, understanding how these emotions are encoded internally remains challenging.
It is even more difficult to objectively name them due to inconsistencies in how humans
perceive and describe emotional experiences. Our most effective way of communicating
emotional states is through facial expressions, which we have learned to interpret quickly and accurately.

This paper presents a face based graphic visualization method for a wide range of emotional displays.
The graphics are linearly parameterized by six dimensions:
pleasure, arousal, dominance, disgust, contempt, and control.
By iteratively presenting generated faces to an AI, a best fit between its
internal state and an artificially elicited emotion can help reveal the AI's emotional state.
Because the graphics are linearly parameterized, there is a strong degree of
consistency between input dimensions, visual display, and numerical representation.
