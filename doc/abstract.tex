The key challenge of affective computing is to translate a subjective emotional experience into
measurable data. Most recent advances in that field have relied on facial expressions as
indicators for inner emotional states.
Some of those have been confirmed as universal in intercultural research.
Many more have been standardized as emoji icons for use in electronic communication.
The problem of these emotional indicators is their categorical nature. There is no
way to measure their distance, or clearly represent any intermediate states that must exist.
For computational purposes the measurement of emotions therefore mostly relies on
dimensional approaches using intependent scales of valence and arousal. However, these methods lack
a consistent mapping to a facial expression that turned out so crucial for the
identification of emotional states.

This paper seeks to solve this problem by introducing a consistent facial visualization
for a five dimensional model. A graphical representation as a comic style facial expression is provided in
examples, explanations and code. It visualizes emotions in exaggerated style
without references to age, gender or race.
The emotional dimensions are borrowed, partly with deviating meaning, from previous models:
valence, arousal, dominance, contempt and control.
Empirical results from crowd workers confirm that the encoded emotional
information can be recorgnized intuitively and without further training.
