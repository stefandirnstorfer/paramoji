A key challenge in human-computer interaction is the understanding and processing of
emotional experiences in terms of measurable data. Emotional states are often
expressed in categories of described subjective feelings. This approach is impractical for
computational purposes, as there is no agreed-upon metric for similarity, distance, or
combinability of such emotions. As a result,
computational processes typically rely on dimensional models using
independent scales like valence, arousal, and dominance. While these systems allow
for precise descriptions of emotional states, there remains a lack of consensus on
how such numerical representations of emotions would actually feel.

This paper presents a method for mapping a numerical emotion model to a graphical
representation of a facial expressions that elicit such emotion when viewed.
Empirical results demonstrate that
untrained crowd workers were able to intuitively translate between the visual
representation and the numerical model. This suggests that the visualization
faithfully conveys some emotional information unique to each set of input values.
The drawing instructions are concise and based entirely on linear transformations,
thus conveying emotions with minimal interpretational complexity.
