\section{Introduction}

Communication over a digital channel.
Faces were used to communicate emotions most efficiently
Early emoticons :( :)
Eastern cultures: oO, ^^.

Unicode standardization
These can be subjectively and intuitively understood by everyone.
Most comprehensive collection of facial emotional expression used in communication.

Emoji are the most widely used system of facial expressions as they are now integrated in electronic devices and part of the unicode standard.
Some emoji seem to be on a scale of increasing intensity, while others seem to be exact negations of each other. However, no clear consensus exists. Some emoji are even interpreted inconsistently accoss cultures

Another system of facial expressions is the Wong-Baker FACES® Pain Rating Scale.
It's medical value and track record are well studied and successfully applied in pain treatment.
In particular they allow consistent pain level assessment.
This system is absolutely clear in its interpretation.
The faces are ordered along an axis and can be turned into statistics, to eg measure an average emotion or its fluctuaton.
This feature make them a very powerful tool in pain therapy, as they satisfy the needs of experts and laymen alike.
However, the scale is only one-dimensional and the range of emotional states is limited to a single aspect of the perceivable space.

Maybe the earliest system of facial visualizations are the Chernoff faces.
These have a very clear numerical interpretation, as each movable part of a human face is controlled by a numeric value.
However, these faces lack an emotional interpretation. It would for example be difficult to define which faciescan be considered to perceive more pain. As such Chernoff faces could be shown to convey no additional information, when compared to other visualization techniques.
\cite{chernoff}

This paper suggests a facial visualization that includes 5 quantitative aspects of a human emotion.
The emotinal dimensions are derived from common psychological models.
The graphics definition is mostly derived from linear combinations of input coordinates.
The contribution of this representation is neither an improved model of emotional representation
nor is it a better set of emoticons.
What it does however contribute is compromise that might strike the right balance between psychological precision, emotional variety and graphical simplicity.

\subsection{Contempt}

Contempt is a separate emotion distinct from anger, disgust or fear.
“I’m better than you and you are lesser than me.”
Combined with high status contempt can be coincide with pleasure.

"This feeling of power and superiority may be a pleasurable experience for some people,
whereas for others it can feel embarrassing and/or shameful."
\cite{contempt_ekman}

Contempt can occur in combination to any of the previously discussed emotions.
Hence, it is a dimension, not a state.

\subsubsection{Extracting a discrete emoji probability}
Unified approach to extract emojis from a finite and a parametric set.

Users would click on early entries in the list
Make the probability distributions appear more uniform than they actually are.

Make results more comparable

\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}_{unicode} =
 \left\{
 \notomoji{2639},
 \notomoji{1f641},
 \notomoji{1f610},
 \cdots
 \right\}
\end{equation}
$sel(n)$ is the selected emoji from a set $E_n$ shown to test participant $n$
\begin{equation}
 c^* =
 \underset{c}{\mathrm{argmax}}
\ \prod_n\frac
 {
  \exp\left(c_{sel(n)} \right)
 }
 {
  \sum_{e\in E_n}\exp(c_e)
 },
 \quad sel(n) \in E_n \subset \mathbb{E}_{unicode}
\end{equation}

We use a vector $c$ to approximate selection probability for the hypothetical
case that $E_n = \mathbb{E}_{unicode}$
\begin{equation}
 \exp(c^*_e) \approx P\left( sel(n) = e \right)
\end{equation}


The winning emoji is $e^*$ that maximizes the odds of being selected.
In the limit this should be the emoji that is selected most often, ie $sel(n)=e^*$ for the most $n$.
\begin{equation}
 e^* = \underset{e}{\mathrm{argmax}}\, c^*_e, \quad e^* \in \mathbb{E}_{unicode}
\end{equation}

This might seem like a complicated way to find the one top matching emoji.
Just counting the emoji by number selected would yield the same result, but don't worry we need a correct proxy for
the actual distribution later.

\subsubsection{Extracting a parametric emoji distribution}
The nice thing about the preivously discussed method is that it directly translates into the selection from
an infinite space of emojis.
The parametric emoji space is generated a vector of 5 dimensions in a unit cube.
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb{E}_{param} = [0,1]^5
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
 \mu^*, \sigma^* =
 \underset{{\mu,\sigma}}{\mathrm{argmax}}
 \prod_n\frac
 {
  \exp\left(\sum_d \frac{(sel(n)_d-\mu_d)^2}{\sigma_d{}^2} \right)
 }
 {
  \sum_{e\in E_n}
  \exp\left(\sum_d \frac{(e_d-\mu_d)^2}{\sigma_d{}^2} \right)
 },
 \quad
 \begin{array}{rl}
  sel(n) \in E_n \subset & \mathbb{E}_{param} \\
  \mu, \mu^* \in & \mathbb{E}_{param}
 \end{array}
\end{equation}

It's important to distinguish $\mu$ from the average selected parameters.
The statistical average is heavily biased against the boundaries where all the interesting emotions are.

Unicode emoji selection accuracy saturates when the best representation is selected.

Parametric emoji increase to refine arbitrarily until a unique parameters are found.
Hence, the real challenge lies in the decoding part.
The question is whether untrained laymen can intuitively make use of this additional precision.

\section{Conclusion}
There are many applications where emotional states need to be captured, processed or displayed.

Smaller emotional scope than emoji, as there exist some emojis for very particular emotions that do not fit in a dimensional model.
The facial expression has fewer dimensions than Chernoff faces which contain dimensions for, eg. size of ears that cannot be varied by people at will.
The dimensions of the presented face are inspired by scientific models, but do they do lack the depth and psychological justification of the eg. the Wong-Baker scale.
However, it is a middle ground between alternative systems.
Thus, it fills a gap that is left open by existing methods.
